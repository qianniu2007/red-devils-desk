What the shape says

Two clubs drew this chart

Under Ferguson the line is almost boring: 21 seasons, 13 titles, an average finish of 1.5th and 83.4 points. The dips are short (1997–98, 2001–02, 2003–05) and always followed by a title. That is what dominance looks like when it is drawn rather than described.

Since 2013 the same chart falls off a shelf. Twelve seasons, no title, an average finish of 5.6th and 65.9 points — a gap of nearly eighteen points a season. The strange part is not the fall, it is the flatness of it: in twelve years there is not one season back above 82 points.

Put the two selections side by side and the argument about “standards” stops being sentimental. It is a nineteen-point gap, measured.
